# 0.2.0

The second iteration of the stackable 3.5" disk bay. It fixes what the first
test print and a drive test fit found, puts the SATA connector on the correct
side, and adds a 2.5" adapter, feet and joiner plates.

**Reprint everything.** Parts from 0.1.0 do not mix with 0.2.0: the rear panel,
bottom plate, walls, caddy tray and bezel have all changed.

## Fixes from the first print and test fit

- **SATA connector on the correct side.** The model had the connector
  mirrored. Seen from behind with the label up, it is at the left, as on a real
  drive. The rear panel's open corner, the bottom plate's rear-panel slots, the
  caddy's push tab and the 2.5" adapter all moved with it.
  ([ADR 0010](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0010-sata-connector-on-the-plus-y-side.md))
- **The caddy now locks into the bay.** The side detent never clicked. It is
  replaced by a leaf spring on the top edge of each caddy wall, which clicks
  into a slot in the top plate and keeps the caddy pressed down and the bezel
  tight against the bay.
  ([ADR 0005](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0005-top-edge-spring-lock-grooved-rear-panel.md))
- **Drives can be taken out again.** Once its pins were in, a drive was almost
  impossible to remove. A service hole next to each finger tip, part of the
  honeycomb pattern, lets a small screwdriver pry the pin out.
  ([ADR 0012](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0012-finger-service-holes.md))
- **The rear panel no longer rattles.** The walls now slide onto the panel's
  side tabs first, so each slot fits its tab exactly.
  ([ADR 0009](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0009-rear-panel-side-tabs-walls-first.md))
- **Tighter press fits.** The bezel tabs and rear panel tabs are 0.08 mm a side
  (0.05 mm across the bezel's floor tab), so they hold without glue.
- **The top plate is propped at the front.** A short lip on the bezel holds up
  the middle of the top plate, and the caddy walls rise almost to the plate at
  the bezel end to hold up its edges. Both stop 0.2 mm short, so they cannot
  bind.
  ([ADR 0011](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0011-bezel-lip-props-the-top-plate.md))

## New

- **Push tab.** The caddy tray reaches through the rear panel's open corner,
  so a caddy can be pushed out from behind.
  ([ADR 0007](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0007-caddy-push-tab-through-sata-corner.md))
- **Finger notch instead of a handle.** The bezel is now a flat plate with a
  drawer-style notch to pull by, and prints with no support.
  ([ADR 0006](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0006-bezel-finger-notch-instead-of-handle.md))
- **2.5" adapter.** A 2.5" HDD or SSD (5 to 15 mm) goes into a normal caddy
  in place of a 3.5" drive, with its connector where a 3.5" drive's would be.
- **Feet** lift the bottom bay off the surface for airflow. Each locks into
  three honeycomb cells.
- **Joiner plates**, in full and half sizes, go between stacked bays or bays
  side by side. They interlock with dovetails, so joints run brick-style
  across bays. ([ADR 0008](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/adr/0008-accessories-adapter-feet-joiners.md))
- **Assembly guide** with pictures for every step:
  [docs/assembly.md](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/assembly.md).

## Known open points

Not yet confirmed on a print:

- whether a drive goes in under the bezel's lip with the bezel already fitted,
  by flexing the bezel
- the moved rear panel opening against real SATA plugs
- the spring lock's click and release force, and whether its 54 mm slit
  bridges cleanly
- whether the new press fits push home by hand

See [What the next print has to answer](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.2.0/docs/design.md#what-the-next-print-has-to-answer).
