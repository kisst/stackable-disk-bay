# 0.3.0

The third iteration of the stackable 3.5" disk bay. It fixes what the 0.2.0
test print found: the walls are now locked into the plates by dovetails, the
caddy is pushed from the middle and pulled by a ledge, and the bezel is held
at all four corners.

**Reprint the plates, walls, caddy tray and bezel.** The rear panel, the 2.5"
adapter, the feet and the joiner plates are unchanged from 0.2.0. The test
coupon gained a vent cell.

## Fixes from the second print

- **The walls no longer come loose.** Each wall tab is a dovetail in a notch
  at the plate's edge, with two angles. A steep 15° flare stops the wall
  lifting out of the plate, the critical load. A shallow 4° splay snaps as
  the wall slides in sideways and stops it sliding back out. The dovetail
  faces have no clearance. The assembly order changes: rear panel into the
  bottom plate, top plate on, then the walls slide in from the sides.
  ([ADR 0014](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.3.0/docs/adr/0014-dovetailed-wall-joint.md))
- **The caddy no longer wedges when pushed out.** The push tab is a
  floor-thick tongue near the middle of the tray's rear edge, 2 mm proud of
  the bay. At the corner, it turned the caddy and jammed it.
  ([ADR 0013](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.3.0/docs/adr/0013-second-print-push-tab-pull-ledge.md))
- **Something to pull by.** With a 3.5" drive in, the finger notch gave
  no grip. The tray's floor tab now runs on 10 mm past the bezel as a pull
  ledge, with a slot for a fingertip or screwdriver. The notch is gone, and
  the bezel is honeycomb all over.
- **Firmer click.** The spring lock's bump reaches 1.5 mm into the top
  plate, up from 1.0 mm.
- **Shorter bezel lip.** It is 10 mm long, centred, where the top plate
  sags most.

## Bezel and caddy

- **The bezel is held at all four corners.** Each caddy wall now has two
  bezel tabs, one at the finger height and one near the top, so the bezel
  can no longer peel forward at the top. Each wall tab's outer face leans
  out 4° and its slot fits that face exactly. The bezel snaps over the tabs
  and is then locked on, together with a 0.4 mm barb on the pull ledge.
  ([ADR 0015](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.3.0/docs/adr/0015-bezel-held-at-four-corners.md))
- **More vents in the caddy walls.** There are two more cells above each
  fixed pin and one beside each finger.

## Checks

- New checks show that a wall lifted or slid outward cuts into the plate,
  and a bezel slid forward cuts into its wall tabs. Each one fails when its
  angle is set to 0.
- The geometry checks and the thin-section check now fail when OpenSCAD
  reports an undefined value, which could quietly drop a feature.
- In the thin-section check, a sloped face no longer shows as a false thin
  sheet.

## Known open points

These have not yet been confirmed on a print:

- whether a wall slides in by hand past its 4° splay, about 0.17 mm at each
  tab end, stays put, and comes out again without damage
- whether the bezel snaps over its wall tabs (0.14 mm) and the barb by hand.
  The lower wall tabs sit where the wall is stiffest
- the firmer spring lock's click, and whether the pull ledge gives enough
  grip against it
- whether the centred push tab pushes the caddy out without wedging
- the snaps are about the size of a printer's tolerance, so the slicer's
  elephant-foot compensation matters

See [What the next print has to answer](https://github.com/kisst/stackable-disk-bay/blob/v0.3.0/docs/design.md#what-the-next-print-has-to-answer).
